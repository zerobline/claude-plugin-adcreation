# Step 5 — Identify the Micro-Tribe

**Codename: The Audience X-Ray + Shadow Matchmaker**

Forget demographics. Forget "target audiences." Build for a **status identity under stress** — a very specific kind of person with a very private tension.

Purpose:

- Make the ad speak **for** someone, not just to them.
- Build psychographic clarity: who they are, what they hide, what they crave.
- Craft an identity-flavoured ad they'll send to a friend without explaining it.

Not "creatives" or "entrepreneurs", but:

- The solo founder who lies about being fully booked.
- The burned-out coach who gives better advice than they take.
- The high-performer whose self-worth collapses without productivity.

**The more specific the tribe, the more scalable the message becomes.**

## 5.1 The Identity Grid — the Audience X-Ray

1. **I am…** — their aspirational self-image ("a creative thinker", "a strong leader").
2. **But I feel…** — their secret fear, shadow, or shame ("…like I'm faking it", "…emotionally scattered").
3. **And I wish…** — their hidden craving or emotional relief ("…I could rest without guilt", "…someone would just get it").

Example:

> I am a visionary coach…
> But I feel like I'm crumbling behind the scenes.
> And I wish I could help others without betraying myself.

This is the **empathy scaffold** — the voice behind the ad.

## 5.2 Match the archetype emotion — the Shadow Matchmaker

| Archetype | Core drive | Shadow emotion |
|---|---|---|
| The Prover | "To be respected" | Fear of being exposed |
| The Hacker | "To beat the system" | Feeling powerless |
| The Escapist | "To feel free" | Feeling trapped |
| The Nurturer | "To help others" | Feeling invisible |
| The Guardian | "To protect what works" | Fear of being irrelevant |

Example: "You're not just a high-performer — you're a Prover running on panic, hoping no one notices you're barely holding it together."

Use the archetype to **flavour the ad's tone and visuals** (Prover → clean, high-status, exposed; Hacker → scrappy, screenshot-native; Escapist → space, air, open composition; Nurturer → warm, quiet, one human detail; Guardian → solid, steady, restrained).

## 5.3 The Recognition Filter — the Mirror Check

Score the tribe on four dimensions (0–5 each):

| Filter | Ask |
|---|---|
| Emotional specificity | Does this feel like one real person's inner monologue? |
| Identity tension | Does it reveal a conflict between who they are and how they feel? |
| Social relatability | Would someone say "this is me — this is us"? |
| Non-job-dependent | Does it transcend labels like "marketer" or "mom"? |

**16–20 = publish-ready tribe.** Below 16? Refine the "But I feel…" line or the shadow emotion.

## Looping back

Step 5 often sharpens the person behind Step 1. When it does, re-read the punchline as that person: if they wouldn't feel caught by it, return to Step 3 with the sharper "But I feel…" line.

For B2B, the tribe is still one person: the buyer, the user, or the person who has to defend the purchase. Pick one and write their Identity Grid; committees don't feel, people do.

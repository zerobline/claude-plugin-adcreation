# Step 6 — Match the Platform

**Codename: The Shape-Shifter + Platform Whisperer**

Even a perfect ad dies if it feels out of place. The job is not to repurpose content — it's to translate **emotion into platform dialect**.

Purpose:

- Adapt the insight so it feels born for the placement.
- Pick the right format, tone, and structure — not just the right words.
- Make people say: "This doesn't feel like an ad… it feels like it belongs here."

The same ad should not feel the same on Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn, email, or a billboard. Each placement is a new emotional language. This step prevents the ad from looking like a repurposed slide deck with a cropped edge.

## 6.1 The Platform Emotion Translator — the Shape-Shifter Grid

| Emotion type | Instagram | TikTok | LinkedIn | Email |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shame / overwhelm | Carousel or Mirror post | POV skit / raw voiceover | Static "pain post" | 3-line confession opener |
| Category flip | Bold Poster / carousel | Before/after reel | "Industry lie" story post | "I used to believe…" opener |
| Identity unlock | One-line poster / reels | Character POV or text-on-face | Hero-journey format | Story → shift → insight arc |

Out-of-home and print:

| Emotion type | Roadside billboard | Street / transit poster | Print |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shame / overwhelm | Mirror Split, two or three words per side | Screenshot Confession or Receipt Stack | Receipt Stack with one line of relief |
| Category flip | Bold Poster with a Contradiction Cut ("Not X. Y.") | Bold Poster + one supporting line | Mirror Split or Bold Poster |
| Identity unlock | Bold Poster: one line + brand | Bold Poster or Cinematic Photo + Text | Cinematic Photo + Text |

Don't ask only "where does this belong?" Ask: *"Where does this truth feel most natural?"*

## 6.2 The Format Selector Grid — the Native Fit Engine

| Insight type | Best native formats | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Shame-based truth | Meme, Mirror Split, screenshot message | Short, raw formats with tension |
| Identity unlock | Bold Poster, mini story, text-layer reel | Centre the phrase; strip the clutter |
| Category flip | Carousel, skit, "confession" static post | Contrast works best in layers |
| Quiet craving | Text meme, message screenshot, email | Feels like a secret being whispered |

Ask:

- "Would this feel native in someone's feed (or on this street)?"
- "Would someone need to explain it to share it?" If yes → rewrite.

## 6.3 Adapt the punchline to platform voice — the Platform Whisperer

| Platform | Voice style | Line plug-in | CTA |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instagram | Relatable + aesthetic | "Ever felt this way?" | "Save this for when it hits again." |
| TikTok | Chaotic, confessional | "If this is you… stitch it." | "Send this to your messy twin." |
| LinkedIn | Vulnerable + wise | "Not everyone will get this — that's okay." | "What's your version of this story?" |
| Email | Low-pressure honesty | "I almost didn't send this…" | "If this hit, reply with 'same.'" |

Add **one native caption or hook** that amplifies the punchline instead of repeating it.

## 6.4 Out-of-home and print rules

Billboards and posters have no caption, no comments, and no second chance. The line is the whole ad.

| Placement | Viewing reality | Line budget | Sign-off / CTA |
|---|---|---|---|
| Roadside billboard | One glance at distance, often in motion | 7 words or fewer | Brand name or logo, optionally a very short URL. No QR codes — nobody can scan at speed. |
| Street, transit, station poster | Seconds to minutes; people wait nearby | About 10 words + one small supporting line | Brand, short URL, or QR code |
| Digital out-of-home screen | A rotating slot of a few seconds | Same as billboard | Each frame must stand alone; never rely on frame two |
| Print (magazine, flyer, direct mail) | Lean-in reading | One-breath headline + up to two short lines | URL, QR code, or offer |

Additional rules:

- **Thumbnail = distance.** If the line can't be read when the mockup is shrunk to about 300 px wide, it can't be read from the road. Enlarge the type or cut words.
- **Location is context.** A billboard can play off where it stands (next to a gym, an office park, a traffic jam). Use this only when the placement is known.
- **Brand small, line huge.** The sign-off should be findable, not competing. One focal point still applies.
- **Two-word halves.** A Mirror Split on a billboard works only if each half reads at a glance.
- **Proof of life.** Out-of-home has no saves or DMs. Pair it with a measurable hook (a unique short URL, a code, a branded search term) and pretest the same creative as a social static.

## Final platform prompt

Complete this sentence for the chosen placement:

> "This tribe feels most **[emotion]** on **[platform]**, so the format should be a **[type]** that delivers **[punchline]** in **[tone]**."

When it sounds like a message that belongs — not a marketing transplant — it's ready to publish.

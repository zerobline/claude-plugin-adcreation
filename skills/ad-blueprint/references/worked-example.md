# Worked example — from intake to billboard

An *illustrative, not empirically validated* autopilot example. Scores are subjective teaching aids; this is not a measured user test. The brand is fictional.

## Intake

- **What:** Steadybook, a cash-flow forecasting app for freelancers. It shows next month's income before it arrives.
- **Who:** freelancers and solo consultants.
- **Where:** a 48-sheet billboard (2:1) on a commuter route through a co-working district, plus an Instagram static.
- **Constraints:** none given. Assumed: English, no brand colour yet, so the accent is red.

## Step 1 — Spike Core

| Candidate | Spec | Hit | Recog | Avg | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| "They want to be seen as in-demand experts… but inside they feel one quiet month away from panic… and you can see it when they refresh their banking app between calls." | 5 | 4 | 5 | 4.7 | **Keep** |
| "They crave freedom, but it's wrapped in a constant hum of money anxiety." | 3 | 4 | 4 | 3.7 | Dies — "hum of anxiety" is vague |
| "Freelancers need better visibility into their cash flow." | 1 | 1 | 2 | 1.3 | Dies — feature talk, no one feels caught |

**Spike Core:** in-demand on the outside, one quiet month from panic on the inside; the tell is refreshing the banking app between calls.

## Step 2 — Belief flip

- Everyone assumes **more clients = more security.**
- It feels safe because hustling is something they can control.
- But it keeps them saying yes to bad projects — and still panicking.

| Expect | Why it fails | We say |
|---|---|---|
| "Get more clients." | The fear is not knowing, not the amount | "You don't need more clients. You need to know next month." |

**Voice:** Confessional Insider. Three ways:
1. One-liner: "Busy isn't safe."
2. Confession: "I had six clients and still checked my bank app ten times a day."
3. Snap: "Fully booked. Fully terrified."

The snap hits fastest.

## Step 3 — Punchline

Weapon: **Shame Mirror** (public behaviour vs. private feeling).

| Version | 3-sec test | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| "Fully booked. Still terrified." | ✅ one breath, stings | 4 words; "still" implies it's a pattern |
| "You're not broke. You're just guessing." | ✅ | Good alternate; softer, more relief |
| "Still refreshing your bank app? Try knowing." | ⚠️ | 7 words, slower; "try knowing" is abstract |
| "Fully booked. Fully terrified." | ✅ | Echo is clever, but cleverness slows the hit |

**Punchline A:** "Fully booked. Still terrified." **Punchline B:** "You're not broke. You're just guessing."

## Step 4 — Visual concept and symbol lock

Shame → **Mirror Split**: left (white) "SAYS — Fully booked." · right (black) "FEELS — Still terrified." Accent red on "terrified." only. Brand sign-off small, bottom right: "Steadybook. Know next month."

Glance Test: get it at a glance ✅ · image extends the line (the split *is* the contradiction) ✅ · premium and clean ✅ · repeatable later ✅ → **4/4**.

**Symbol Lock:** split between public success and private worry; category-confusion risk: fully booked could imply a hotel. Final mockup: generated after Step 6 using the Mirror Split template in `render-mockup.md`.

## Step 5 — Micro-tribe

- **I am** a sought-after independent designer…
- **But I feel** one slow month away from going back to a job…
- **And I wish** I could see the money coming, so I could stop checking.

Archetype: **The Prover** (fear of being exposed). Recognition Filter: specificity 5, tension 5, relatability 4, non-job-dependent 4 = **18/20** → publish-ready. The punchline still mirrors this person; no loop back needed.

## Step 6 — Platform

- **Billboard:** no caption; the split is the ad. 4 words + brand. No QR code.
- **Instagram (4:5 static):** same Mirror Split stacked vertically (SAYS on top, FEELS below). Caption: "Ever said 'fully booked' with your stomach in a knot? Save this for the next quiet month."
- Final prompt: "This tribe feels most exposed on their commute past the co-working district, so the format should be a Mirror Split billboard that delivers 'Fully booked. Still terrified.' in a confessional, deadpan tone."

## Step 7 — Taste test

| Clarity | Surprise | Emotion | Recognition | Visual | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 4 | 5 | 5 | 4 | **23/25 → Audience-test candidate (subjective)** |

Surprise is a 4, not a 5: Mirror Splits are a known device; the line, not the layout, carries the surprise.

**2×2:**

| | Mirror Split | Bold Poster |
|---|---|---|
| "Fully booked. Still terrified." | A/A — lead | A/B — strong, loses the "says/feels" twist |
| "You're not broke. You're just guessing." | B/A — split feels forced | B/B — best for Instagram, more relief |

**Reality Loop:** show A/A and B/B for three seconds at thumbnail size to five freelancers; ask what it said and what they felt. Run B/B as an Instagram static in the same city first and track saves and shares. For the billboard, use a unique short URL to measure response.

**Biggest risk:** non-freelancers may read "fully booked" as a hotel or restaurant. Fix if the test shows confusion: "Fully booked clients. Still terrified." costs one word and the rhythm, so test before changing.

# Step 4 — Design the Visual Twist

**Codename: Style Mapper + Chokehold Constraints**

If the punchline is the weapon, the visual is the **trigger**.

Purpose:

- Translate emotion into visual structure, not decoration.
- Choose a format that feels like the only way this idea could exist.
- Apply constraints that force emotional clarity, not design fluff.

Don't decorate the punchline. **Deliver it.** A powerful ad is felt in the first 1.5 seconds of a scroll (or the first glance from a moving car).

## 4.1 Match format to feeling — the Style Mapper

| Emotion | Best visual format | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Shame / guilt | Mirror Split (before/after) | "Pretend vs. Reality" layout |
| Sarcasm / rebellion | Screenshot / meme | iMessage- or post-style frame |
| Overwhelm | Browser tabs / Receipt Stack | Chaotic UI with one clear line |
| Identity shift | Bold Poster | Black/white field, massive type |
| Exhaustion, longing, relief, belonging | The Human Moment (vector figure or photo) | One person, one telling pose, one prop in the accent colour |
| Emotional journey | The Human Moment (photo) + text layer | A candid moment + a soft, shocking truth |

Ask: "Could they *feel the point* even if they muted it or skipped the caption?"

### Words or a body?

Before picking a format, ask where the sting lives:

- **In the words** ("Fully booked. Still terrified.") → type-led: Bold Poster, Mirror Split, Screenshot Confession.
- **In a body** (slumped shoulders at 5:30 a.m., a hand hovering over "send", a back turned to a crowded room) → people-led: the Human Moment. The pose says the truth; the line only names it.
- **In a thing** (a cold coffee, forty open tabs, a cracked screen) → object metaphor or Receipt Stack.

When people would make the ad land harder, show people. Don't default to type just because type is easier to render.

## 4.2 The Chokehold Constraints — clarity through constraint

Creativity loves boundaries. Limit the choices; increase the punch.

1. **Colour rule** — black/white + one bold accent (red, neon green, electric blue). Forces visual focus. If the brand has a colour, the brand colour becomes the accent.
2. **Layout rule** — one focal point only (the punchline, an object, or a shape). No distractions.
3. **Image rule** — metaphor, screenshot, or one *specific* person in a telling moment — never stock faces (posed, smiling, generic, looking at the camera). A broken screen beats a sad model; a nurse's slumped shoulders over a coffee at 5:30 a.m. beats both.
4. **Type rule** — one clean font, no gradients.

## 4.3 Symbol Lock (before rendering)

Name **one recurring symbol** (object, person gesture, strong split or metaphor). Explain its real product relationship, what a cold passer-by would read, the risk of category confusion, and why it isn't merely a competitor's recognisable symbol. Check it works at street distance and in mobile formats. This is a creative distinctiveness check, not legal trademark clearance. The final visual is rendered **after Step 6** fixes audience, placement and design constraints; Step 4 produces the composition plan.

## 4.4 The Scroll-Test — the 1.5-second filter

| Question | Pass if |
|---|---|
| Get it in 1.5 seconds? | Message is obvious at a glance |
| Does the image echo the punchline? | Design *extends* the line, not just matches it |
| Could this be a top-tier brand ad? | Feels premium, clean, confident |
| Would someone screenshot this? | Emotional clarity + visual power |

3+ of 4 = ship it. Missing more than 2 = strip, simplify, sharpen.

**Glance Test (out-of-home variant):** replace "1.5 seconds" with "one glance from a moving car or a passing walk, at distance", and replace "screenshot" with "would they repeat the line to someone later?"

## The Visual Format Vault — frame the feeling

Great ads don't explain themselves; they hit before the words are read.

### Format 1 — The Bold Poster

- **Emotion:** identity shift, mic-drop truth.
- **Style:** one line, massive type, zero fluff.
- **Template:** black or white background; big sans-serif (Inter, Helvetica, Poppins); no clutter — on social, not even a logo; on paid placements, a small brand sign-off only.
- **Example:** "You're not behind. You're building in silence."
- **Use when** the punchline is so strong that visuals would dilute it. The strongest default for roadside billboards.

### Format 2 — The Screenshot Confession

- **Emotion:** shame, overthinking, guilt.
- **Style:** an invented iMessage, notes-app page, chat, or social post.
- **Template:** looks like a real conversation they'd never post; lowercase, timestamps, or "me:" format.
- **Example:** "me: I can't rest unless I've earned it. also me: always tired. never enough."
- **Use when** the insight sounds like a private text thread. Works on social, transit, and street posters (people have time to read); too dense for roadside billboards.
- Generic UI only — no real app logos, real usernames, or real people.

### Format 3 — The Mirror Split

- **Emotion:** emotional contrast, "pretend vs. panic".
- **Style:** side-by-side halves.
- **Template:** left = what they show/say; right = what they feel/hide.
- **Example:** Left "Says: Just another Monday" · Right "Feels: I can't do this again".
- **Use to** expose the truth behind the persona. Works on billboards when each half carries two or three words.

### Format 4 — The Receipt Stack

- **Emotion:** overwhelm, chaos, burnout.
- **Style:** layered browser tabs, sticky notes, calendar blocks, a to-do stack.
- **Template:** visual clutter → one line of clarity at the bottom.
- **Example:** stacked tabs "CRM login" / "Inspo reel" / "Google Doc: PLAN A" → "You don't need more tools. You need less panic."
- **Use when** stress is the visual story, not just the message. The clutter must read as texture, the line as the only thing to read.

### Format 5 — The Meme Flip

- **Emotion:** rebellion, irony, sarcasm.
- **Style:** a familiar visual grammar + a truth overlay that reframes it.
- **Example:** distracted-boyfriend structure — "Me → planning a new funnel instead of fixing the one that's broken."
- **Use to** grab attention and twist a familiar image into emotional clarity.
- Famous meme photos are usually copyrighted. Recreate the *structure* with original or licensed imagery, or simple illustrated shapes.

### Format 6 — The Human Moment

- **Emotion:** exhaustion, longing, relief, belonging, quiet pride — any truth that lives in a body.
- **Style:** one person, one telling pose, one prop. The figure is big and cropped (often bleeding off the bottom edge); the line sits in the empty space beside it.
- **Two renderings:**
  - **Vector figure** — a bold, pictogram-style person drawn directly in the SVG: big geometric shapes, a silhouette that reads at distance, the prop in the accent colour. Always available; strongest on billboards and posters. Construction rules in `render-mockup.md`.
  - **Photo** — a candid, documentary-style photo of one person. Write an image brief (template in `render-mockup.md`) the user can generate in any image tool or shoot; build the layout around a sized placeholder; composite the photo, converted to black and white, when the user provides it.
- **Example:** a night-shift nurse in profile, eyes closed, holding a steaming cup (accent) → "Your night ends here." · "[Café name] · Open 05:30".
- **Use when** the answer to "words or a body?" is *body*. Brief the person as a moment and a tell ("hands wrapped around a cup, still in scrubs, eyes closed"), never as "person looking stressed".
- People are specific, respectfully drawn, and fictional: characterise through pose, clothing, and props, not caricatured features. Never depict a real, identifiable person without their consent.

## Design constraint recap

- **Colour:** black and white + one accent.
- **Font:** one clean font. No gradients.
- **Focal point:** one message, one moment. Strip the rest.

These formats exist to **force clarity**, not to showcase creativity.

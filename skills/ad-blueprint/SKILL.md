# Ad Blueprint — the Mastermind 7-step method

Most ads don't fail because they're ugly, too long, or missing a CTA. They fail because they don't make anyone *feel* anything. The job is not to win attention but to win **recognition**: one line and one image that make the audience flinch, nod, or whisper "that's me."

Build every ad from one raw emotional truth outward. Features and benefits come last, if at all.

```
Clarify → Flip → Write → Design → Tribe → Platform → Taste Test
(truth)   (belief) (line)  (visual) (who)   (where)    (score & iterate)
```

## Before step 1: intake

Collect only what is missing, in one short message:

- **What** is advertised, and the one thing it changes for people.
- **Who** it is for — any rough description is enough; Step 5 sharpens it.
- **Where** it runs: roadside billboard, street or transit poster, digital screen, print, Instagram, LinkedIn, TikTok cover, email, or several. Size or format if known.
- **Hard constraints**: brand colours, logo, mandatory text or legal lines, language, tone no-gos.

If the request already answers these, skip the questions and state assumptions in one line.

**Pick a mode.**

- **Autopilot** (default when the user just wants the ad): run every step, show each step's shortlist and pick in a compact block, then deliver the build sheet and mockup. "Just do it" means no questions, not fewer steps: Step 7's scoring, cold critique, and 2×2 still run (render only the lead variant unless asked for all four).
- **Guided** (when the user wants to co-create, learn the method, or says "walk me through it"): pause at three checkpoints and let the user choose — the Spike Core (end of Step 1), the punchline (end of Step 3), and the visual format (end of Step 4).

## Step 1 — Clarify the emotional truth (the Spike Core)

Find the unspoken contradiction the audience lives in and hides. If it doesn't sting, it doesn't sell.

1. Write at least three truth lines with the **Shame Scanner** ("They want to be seen as… but inside they feel… and you can see it when they…") and the **Contradiction Stack** templates.
2. Rate each line with the **Spike Meter**: Specificity, Emotional Hit, Self-Recognition, 1–5 each.
3. Keep only lines averaging **4+**. The one that stings most becomes the **Spike Core** — the emotional engine of the ad.
4. If nothing reaches 4, sharpen the *behavioural tell* (the observable thing they do), or calibrate against the Spike Vault.

Details and examples: `references/step-1-emotional-truth.md`. Calibration lines by audience: `references/spike-vault.md`.

## Step 2 — Flip the category logic

Disrupt the belief system behind the category, not the product.

1. Run the **Default Belief Scanner** three times: "Everyone assumes… / That feels safe because… / But it's actually keeping them…". Pick the one that feels obvious but has never been said like this.
2. Fill the **Reframe Reactor Grid**: what they expect → why it fails → what we say instead. The best flip feels like liberation, not opposition.
3. Choose a **Rebel Archetype** voice (Underdog Hacker, Confessional Insider, Myth Buster, Shadow Healer, Soft Rebel). Test: would the audience trust this voice with their shame?
4. Say the flip three ways — punchy one-liner, soft confession, myth-busting snap. Keep the one that hits hardest and fastest.

Details: `references/step-2-category-flip.md`.

## Step 3 — Write a sticky punchline

Turn the truth and the flip into one line that interrupts. Short, specific, strangely true, impossible to ignore.

1. Choose **one emotional weapon**: Shame Mirror, Contradiction Cut, or Identity Unlock.
2. Write at least three versions with the templates ("Not [thing]. [Truth]." / "You're not [X]. You're just [truth]." / "Still [problem]? Try [new mindset]."), then free variations.
3. **3-Second Truth Test**: it lands in a single breath, and it is felt, not just understood. Cut anything that fails.
4. **Micro-Loop**: read it aloud, imagine a cold reader, remove one word and see if it hits harder.
5. Final filter: would someone screenshot this and send it to a struggling friend?

For roadside billboards the line gets **7 words or fewer**. Details: `references/step-3-punchline.md`.

## Step 4 — Design the visual twist

The punchline is the weapon; the visual is the trigger. Translate emotion into structure, not decoration.

1. **Style Mapper**: match the emotion to a format (shame → Mirror Split, sarcasm → Screenshot/Meme, overwhelm → Receipt Stack, identity shift → Bold Poster, emotional journey → Cinematic Photo + Text).
2. Build from the **Visual Format Vault**: Bold Poster, Screenshot Confession, Mirror Split, Receipt Stack, Meme Flip.
3. Apply the **Chokehold Constraints**: black/white + one bold accent; one focal point; metaphor or screenshot, never stock faces; one clean font, no gradients.
4. Run the **Scroll-Test** (or the Glance Test for out-of-home): 3+ of 4 = ship; missing more than 2 = strip, simplify, sharpen.
5. **Render a mockup** following `references/render-mockup.md`.

Details: `references/step-4-visual-twist.md`.

## Step 5 — Identify the micro-tribe

Forget demographics. Build for a status identity under stress — one specific person with one private tension.

1. Fill the **Identity Grid**: "I am… / But I feel… / And I wish…".
2. Pair the tribe with a **Shadow Matchmaker** archetype (Prover, Hacker, Escapist, Nurturer, Guardian) to flavour tone and visuals.
3. Score with the **Recognition Filter** (4 dimensions, 0–5 each). 16–20 = publish-ready tribe; below 16, rework the "But I feel…" line.
4. If the sharper tribe no longer matches the punchline, loop back to Step 3 before moving on.

Details: `references/step-5-micro-tribe.md`.

## Step 6 — Match the platform

Translate emotion into the placement's dialect; never just resize.

1. Use the **Platform Emotion Translator** and **Format Selector Grid** to pick the container for this placement.
2. For social and email, add **one native caption or hook** that amplifies the punchline instead of repeating it (Platform Whisperer).
3. For billboards, posters, and print there is no caption: the line *is* the ad, plus a small brand sign-off. Apply the out-of-home rules in the reference.
4. Complete the **Final Platform Prompt**: "This tribe feels most [emotion] on [platform], so the format should be a [type] that delivers [punchline] in [tone]."

Details, including billboard, poster, and print placements: `references/step-6-platform.md`.

## Step 7 — Taste test and iteration loop

Most "pretty good" ads settle before they sting. Score, collide variants, and get cold eyes.

1. Score the ad on the **5D Taste Calibration Grid** — Clarity, Surprise, Emotion, Recognition, Visual Power (1–5 each). **20–25** publish; **16–19** tweak one visual, one word, one tone; **below 16** rebuild from the Spike Core.
2. Get a **cold critique**. When the `cold-creative-director` agent is available, give it only the finished ad (line, mockup path or description, placement) and a one-sentence audience — never the reasoning behind it. Otherwise switch into the stance of the `ad-critique` skill and judge the ad as if seeing it for the first time.
3. Build the **A/B War Room 2×2**: Punchline A/B × Visual A/B. Keep the one or two with the fastest hit and cleanest silhouette.
4. Hand the user the **Reality Loop**: peer provocation questions and a micro-audience test. Track saves, DMs, replies, and shares — not likes.

Details: `references/step-7-taste-test.md`.

## Score like a cold-blooded ad director

Self-scores inflate; inflated scores ship weak ads.

- A **5** means diary-worthy — it feels like a secret being exposed. Compare against the 🔴 lines in the Spike Vault before awarding one.
- A line that names a feature or a generic benefit scores **3 or less** on Emotional Hit.
- Show the losing candidates and one-line reasons they lost. Seeing weak next to strong is how the user's taste gets trained.
- Never round up to pass a gate. If nothing passes, say so and loop back.

## Guardrails — mirror, don't wound

- Expose a truth the audience recognises in themselves; never mock or demean them. The truth should end in relief, recognition, or permission.
- Take extra care with bodies, health, money trouble, grief, and anything aimed at children: the line must loosen the wound, not press on it. Check the category's advertising rules (health, finance, alcohol, gambling, children).
- No fake proof: no invented testimonials, reviews, statistics, or claims the product can't back up.
- Screenshot and meme formats: write an invented conversation; never impersonate real people, brands, or accounts. Use original or licensed images — popular meme templates and stock photos are often copyrighted.
- "Could this be a Nike or Apple ad?" is a quality bar for confidence and cleanliness, never a cue to borrow anyone's marks, slogans, or look.

## Deliverable: the Ad Build Sheet

Finish with a build sheet plus the mockup file. Keep it scannable:

```markdown
# Ad Build Sheet — [product] · [placement]

**Spike Core** (Spike Meter x.x): …
**Belief flip:** Everyone assumes … → We say: …  · Voice: [archetype]
**Punchline:** "…"  (weapon: …)
  Alternates: "…" · "…"
**Micro-tribe:** I am … / But I feel … / And I wish …  · Archetype: …  · Recognition x/20
**Visual:** [format] · palette [black/white + accent] · focal point: … · Scroll/Glance test x/4
**Placement:** [platform, size] · caption/sign-off: … · CTA: …
**5D Taste score:** Clarity x · Surprise x · Emotion x · Recognition x · Visual x = xx/25 → [Publish | Tweak | Rebuild]
**2×2 variants:** A/A … · A/B … · B/A … · B/B …
**Reality Loop:** who to show it to, what to ask, what to track
**Mockup:** [file path or inline preview]
```

Close with one sentence on the single biggest risk in the ad (the place a cold reader is most likely to miss it) and the one change that would push its score higher.
